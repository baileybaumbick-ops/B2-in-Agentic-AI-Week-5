# Moneyball Case

The Oakland A's found that on-base percentage predicted wins better than slugging, leading to an arbitrage opportunity that eventually converged toward market equilibrium.

## Key Points
- Scouts valued players partly on looks (the "Good Face"). ([[Class 1 - Introduction to Strategy#Case - Moneyball|Class 1 › Case - Moneyball]])
- On-base percentage predicted wins better than slugging, yet sluggers were paid far more. ([[Class 1 - Introduction to Strategy#Case - Moneyball|Class 1 › Case - Moneyball]])
- The market price and the true value of OBP converged by about 2004-2005 as markets moved toward equilibrium. ([[Class 1 - Introduction to Strategy#Case - Moneyball|Class 1 › Case - Moneyball]])
