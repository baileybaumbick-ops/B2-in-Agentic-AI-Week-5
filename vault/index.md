# Strategy Wiki

Concept notes built from my Strategy course study notes. Each note links to related notes and back to the original source section it came from. The originals are listed in [[Source Catalog]].

## Strategy Foundations
- [[Economic Approach]] - Economics frames decisions by identifying decision makers, goals, choices, and outputs, moving from description to prediction to prescription.
- [[Profit Function]] - Profit is calculated as (price - cost per unit) multiplied by the quantity sold.
- [[Repeated Interactions]] - Repeated interactions allow firms to build reputations and cooperate by threatening retaliation over the long term.
- [[Strategic Context]] - The context includes various factors like buyers, competitors, legal limits, and internal assets that influence outcomes.
- [[Strategy]] - Strategy is a plan to achieve a firm's goal by establishing a persistent difference over rivals.
- [[Trade-Offs and Opportunity Cost]] - Firms must manage trade-offs where moving one goal affects another, and opportunity cost is the value of the best alternative foregone.

## Value and Advantage
- [[AAA Framework]] - The AAA framework links assets, activities, and advantage, where assets enable activities, and activities create advantage when they fit the context.
- [[Added Value]] - Added value is the total value created by a firm minus the value created without it, representing what others would pay to keep the firm.
- [[Competitive Advantage]] - Competitive advantage is the ability to be consistently more profitable than rivals in a market.
- [[Cost-Based Advantage]] - Cost-based advantage is achieved by optimizing costs, such as using data or strategic investments to achieve operational excellence.
- [[Counter-Positioning]] - Counter-positioning involves choosing a market position that the incumbent does not wish to fight over, thereby avoiding costly conflict with existing customers.
- [[Joint Venture Exercise]] - Partnerships can be structured to maximize value, where the three-firm venture creates the most value.
- [[Niche-Based Advantage]] - Niche-based advantage involves finding a subset of customers where price rivalry is reduced, often through differentiation.
- [[Public Goods]] - Space debris is a negative externality, and cleanup is a public good.
- [[Value Creation]] - Value created is the gap between a customer's benefit and the cost.
- [[Value Proposition]] - The value proposition is a statement detailing how a firm intends to create value and bridge the gap between customer benefits and costs.

## Industry Analysis
- [[Five Forces]] - Porter's Five Forces is a framework used to analyze how much value an industry can create and how much firms can keep as profit.
- [[Pricing Game]] - Algorithmic pricing can be used to sustain high prices without explicit agreement, complicating antitrust enforcement.
- [[Rivalry]] - Rivalry describes the intensity of competition among current rivals, and price rivalry is a major factor in determining profit.

## Entry and Competition
- [[Dynamic Entry Games]] - This framework analyzes how incumbents will respond to an entrant's decision to enter, using backward induction to predict strategic responses.
- [[Entry Barriers]] - Barriers to entry make a market difficult to enter or less profitable for new competitors.
- [[Entry Deterrence]] - Incumbents can keep competitors out by changing the payoffs before entry occurs.
- [[Entry and Exit]] - Entry and exit are common occurrences where entrants and exiting firms are generally smaller than established firms.
- [[Water Bottle Exercise]] - This exercise illustrates how positioning can influence outcomes when an entrant challenges an incumbent's offering.

## Company Cases
- [[CF Industries Case]] - CF created value by controlling the supply chain, storage, and pricing information for fertilizer, using regulatory barriers to capture market advantage.
- [[Cola Wars Case]] - The Cola Wars illustrates how Coke and Pepsi shaped competition by focusing on advertising and differentiation rather than price wars.
- [[Intel Case]] - Intel's strategy shifted from DRAM to microprocessors, demonstrating how network effects and IP protection drive added value.
- [[Moneyball Case]] - The Oakland A's found that on-base percentage predicted wins better than slugging, leading to an arbitrage opportunity that eventually converged toward market equilibrium.
- [[Tesla Case]] - Tesla demonstrated how learning-curve and scale effects can overcome initial financial disadvantages, leveraging technological advantages to overcome high entry barriers.
