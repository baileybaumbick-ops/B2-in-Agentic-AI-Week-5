# Five Forces

Porter's Five Forces is a framework used to analyze how much value an industry can create and how much firms can keep as profit. It serves as a checklist to understand the context of competition.

## Key Points
- The five forces are substitutes, buyer power, supplier power, entry, and rivalry. ([[Class 4 - Industry Analysis#Five Forces as a starting point|Class 4 › Five Forces as a starting point]])
- Substitutes meet the same need as your product and affect profit similarly to an entrant with a different product. ([[Class 4 - Industry Analysis#Substitutes|Class 4 › Substitutes]])
- Buyers can push prices down when they are few and large, when there are many substitutes, or when they can bypass the seller. ([[Class 4 - Industry Analysis#Buyer power and supplier power|Class 4 › Buyer power and supplier power]])
- Supplier power is high when suppliers face little competition and you cannot easily make the input yourself. ([[Class 4 - Industry Analysis#Buyer power and supplier power|Class 4 › Buyer power and supplier power]])
